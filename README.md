In-house Computer Assisted Design (CAD) and 3D printing capabilities using Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) 
allow for rapid prototyping of useful laboratory items.  This capability can also support the 
development of disability compliant model revisions. This approach benefits from Just-In-Time (JIT) production.  
We address a safety related engineering control for a “sharps” risk (carrying multiple glass slide racks).  
Automated Immunohistochemistry platforms require racks to hold multiple microscope slides, during 
an automated staining protocol.  
The glass microscope slides are considered a potential “sharp” and must be transported to and from 
the automated staining platform with caution.  
Dropping or damaging the slides can lead to loss of irreplaceable samples as well as the potential 
hazard associated with broken glass.  
We have designed a 3D printable multiple slide rack carrier to safely transport multiple racks of glass slides. 
The design is 3D printable, using a biodegradable and non-toxic material, aligned with our 
laboratory Green Certification.  
Pilot testing with user feedback led to a stable, secure and convenient form factor for transporting 
multiple slide racks.  
The parametric file for downloading and 3D printing this device is provided in a public link.    

The laboratory safety committee reviews all active Standard Operating Procedures that relate to  Safety, as 
well as performing periodic observations of workflow, operations and new procedures.  One procedure which became a 
focus of critical attention was the transport of multiple full glass slide racks in the laboratory.  

In a high volume IHC laboratory, using automated staining platforms, it is often necessary to transport multiple 
slide racks to a staining platform. When carrying more than one full rack, there is an inherent risk of dropping 
a slide rack when transporting them in the laboratory.  This raises several safety concerns associated with glass 
slide breakage. Following a review of the associated risk-topology, a slide-rack carrier was considered appropriate 
as a “laboratory engineering control”.  We subsequently defined the parameters for a prototype multiple slide 
rack carrier. 

Our laboratory is “Green Level 5 Certified” so it was felt that the final versions, which did not require 
significant chemical or impact resistance, pliability or stability under ultraviolet light could be printed 
using a biodegradable and non-toxic Polylactic Acid (PLA).  PLA is an eco-friendly thermoplastic derived 
from renewable resources like corn starch, cassava, or sugarcane. 


The printer uses a standard 0.4 mm nozzle for the BambuLab X2D printer.  The model requires approximately 
(depending on the variation being printed) 29 meters of filament, weighing 87 grams, and takes 3 hours 
to print.  The printed model  measures 97x166x147 mm with a volume of 1322374 mm^3. 

 Instructions for use for the slide rack carrier:

Inspect the slide rack for damage or wear before using
Load a slide rack with glass microscope slides
Place the rack carrier on a flat surface, away from a table edge.
Load the rack carrier with multiple slide racks on either side of the handle 
Balance multiple slide racks on the carrier
Hold the rack by the provided handle and transport the racks.
Unload the rack carrier one slide rack at a time. 

Incorporating a safety team evaluation, and user feedback, we can provide near real time 
rapid prototype engineering controls that address laboratory safety challenges.  This approach 
can also address individual preferences and even disability responsive revisions to model designs.  
In this paper we describe the design, revision, prototyping and small run JIT  production of an 
engineering control for safely carrying multiple slide racks.

Not surprisingly, staff felt that each design that they were asked to evaluate had specific 
advantages and chose to recommend that all design variations be made available based on personal preference.  

The carrier rack can be conveniently designed in open source CAD software (FreeCAD), quickly  
3D printed, with multiple design variations, using standard FDM on a Bambu Lab X2D printer, 
from a biodegradable non-toxic material (PLA).
